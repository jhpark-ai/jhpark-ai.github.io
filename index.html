I am an AI researcher at TelePIX working on computer vision and the learning problems underneath it, from implicit neural representations to few-shot adaptation and satellite perception. My main line of work asks how pretrained visual representations transfer and adapt to new tasks, and how far those methods carry into remote sensing and spaceflight.

  • Email
  • Google Scholar

  • LinkedIn

I previously spent four years on neural surrogate modeling for semiconductor devices, working on knowledge transfer between devices and on sample-efficient learning from limited measurement data. Much of my current research revisits those problems in a visual setting.

I am particularly interested in methods that adapt existing models efficiently and remain reliable outside the settings they were built for.

Research

the four basins of fig. 1

🎯basin 1

Transfer & adaptation

How pretrained visual representations carry over to new tasks with little data or compute: few-shot and test-time adaptation, task arithmetic across models, and training-free methods on frozen backbones.

🧠basin 2

Neural representations & operators

Implicit neural representations and neural operators as compact function approximators: closed-form fitting, physics-informed operators for scientific data, and neural compact models of semiconductor devices.

🌏basin 3

Remote sensing

Machine learning for Earth-observation imagery in RGB, multispectral and hyperspectral bands, much of it built to run on the satellite: compression and vision-language summaries before downlink, few-shot open-vocabulary segmentation, methane detection and rapid disaster mapping.

🛰️basin 4

Spacecraft pose estimation

6-DoF pose estimation of spacecraft from onboard cameras: multi-satellite benchmarks and sequential datasets, and latent filtering that keeps the estimate stable over time.
